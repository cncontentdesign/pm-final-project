> _The personal reflection that closes your final project._

## Friction points

The hardest part was the tension between the user and the buyer. Anything good for the frontline coordinator risks touching the backend depth the admins actually pay for, so I carried guardrails (Board and Optimizer adoption, manager CSAT) the whole way to stop myself solving one side at the other's expense. It's the exact thing I wrestle with in my day job, where the people using the product and the people signing off on it rarely want the same thing. Naming that trade-off up front, rather than finding it after launch, was the useful bit.

## Key learnings

How much the WON'T HAVE list did for the project. The decisions that protected this launch were the things I said no to. Cutting compliance-flag alerts, even though they're half the feature's name, kept the pilot small enough to actually ship and measure. I'd never really thought of "no" as the deliverable before.

## Aha! moment

How fast we could pull together a pile of messy, disparate inputs and make sense of them. Bugs, interview quotes, funnel numbers, CSAT, churn notes, all in different shapes. Normally that's days of stitching. With AI in the loop I could collate it and reconcile the qual and the quant in a fraction of the time, and that's what let me drill past the noise to one cheap, specific win rather than a vague "the product feels bloated".
