Strategy · Problem & Value

The screen built for truth became the one they disbelieve

The problem hook

Fleet coordinators no longer trust their own dispatch board. A reassignment takes 8–15 minutes to reach a driver with no push, and delivered stops still read "in progress" an hour later. So ~69% abandon the tool right after assigning routes, daily time lost to WhatsApp workarounds has tripled to 31 minutes, and Coordinator NPS has cratered 30 points to -12. The one screen built to give real-time truth has become the screen they've learned to disbelieve — and leaner rivals are already circling our largest enterprise contracts.

Value proposition

For frontline fleet coordinators at enterprise accounts, we will deliver RouteLogic Velocity, a streamlined interface that eliminates operational noise and manual workarounds while preserving backend platform power, because key accounts are actively trialing leaner rivals, putting our largest enterprise contracts at immediate risk.

The data-backed hypothesis

Delivering real-time trust in the core of the product — instant, confirmed reassignments and accurate stop status that matches the ground — will pull live coordination back onto the platform for mid-size 3PL coordinators and cut daily wasted time in half.

North-star: dispatch-workflow completion (Open → Shift Handoff) climbs 31% → 65%+. Guardrails held: Board 91%, Optimizer 85%, Manager Reporting CSAT 4.5. Go/no-go after a 90-day pilot on 2–3 enterprise accounts — targeting those most at risk of churning.
SLIDE 6 / 10
Research · Competitive Workaround & Journey

The rival isn't a product — it's the WhatsApp group

Who / Goal / Friction

Who — a mid-size 3PL dispatcher keeping a live fleet routed correctly and knowing, in real time, where every stop stands.

Goal — reassign routes and read accurate delivery status off a single trustworthy board, without a phone in the other hand.

Friction — the board lies to them. Reassignments take 8–15 min with no push (BUG-2044); completed stops read "in progress" for up to an hour (BUG-2072).

External tools they're forced into (the workaround)
WhatsApp group = "the real system" (UXR-02) Direct voice calls to office/drivers (UXR-03) Inbound driver texts, manually logged (UXR-01)

They run WhatsApp and the app as two parallel systems all shift — make the change in-app, distrust it, re-send over WhatsApp, wait for a manual reply, then ping each driver to confirm true stop status.

The evidence that proves it

"We keep a WhatsApp group as the real system." — UXR-02

"A stop shows 'in progress' when it was delivered an hour ago. I can't trust the board." — UXR-09

Supporting bugs: BUG-2044 (reassign 8–15 min, no push) · BUG-2072 (status lag 20–60 min)

Competitive read

Complexity was cited by 4 of 5 churning accounts (was 1 of 8). Leaner rivals are actively trialed on our largest, most at-risk enterprise contracts — the threat is not a feature gap, it's the trust gap that pushed coordination off-platform.

PRD highlights — B6 Driver Alert Notifications

Vision — every reassignment reaches the affected driver and comes back confirmed in under a minute, so the coordinator trusts the board and never opens WhatsApp to check.

Screen 1 · Entry

Coordinator Board / Reassign — route list, status chips, driver picker, confirm modal.

Screen 2 · Core

Driver Alert (phone) — simulated push, change summary, new stop + ETA, big "Got it".

Screen 3 · Success

Ack Tracker — Sent→Delivered→Acknowledged, time-to-ack, "Confirmed — no follow-up".

Made explicit that a vague brief would miss: the alert isn't complete without a confirmed acknowledgement the coordinator can see on the board. The misery isn't the missing notification — it's not knowing the driver saw the change.
Gap the prototype revealed: "amber = not acknowledged" fails on screen — you can't tell a 10-second wait from a 10-minute one. A "time since sent" signal is a Must.

SLIDE 8 / 10
Validation · A/B Experiment Brief

One experiment, one number, one fixed read date

Hypothesis

I believe Driver Alert Notifications for a mid-size 3PL Coordinator will bring live coordination onto the platform and cut daily wasted time in half — as measured by a 15% change in reassignment→acknowledgement time (8–15 min → <60s) within 90 days. We will protect Coordinator core-feature adoption (Board 91%, Optimizer 85%) throughout.

Control (A)

The board lies. Reassignment reaches the driver in 8–15 min with no push (BUG-2044); stops read "in progress" up to an hour → ~69% post-assignment abandonment.

Variant (B)

Commit fires a targeted alert to the affected driver alone; board = Sent <2s. Card shows what changed + ETA + timestamp; one "Got it" advances Sent→Delivered→Acknowledged on the board — no WhatsApp or phone step.

Held constant (isolation check)
Open RouteLogic· Load Fleet View· Assign via Optimizer· Log Compliance· Shift Handoff

All earlier and later steps in the workflow are unchanged.

SLIDE 10 / 10
Story · Reflection & What's Next

Friction, the aha, and the road ahead

Friction points

The hardest part was the tension between the user and the buyer. Anything good for the frontline coordinator risks touching the backend depth admins pay for, so I carried guardrails (Board & Optimizer adoption, manager CSAT) the whole way. Naming that trade-off up front — rather than finding it after launch — was the useful bit.

Key learning

How much the WON'T HAVE list did for the project. The decisions that protected this launch were the things I said no to. Cutting compliance-flag alerts — even though they're half the feature's name — kept the pilot small enough to actually ship and measure. I'd never thought of "no" as the deliverable before.

Aha! moment

How fast we could pull a pile of messy, disparate inputs — bugs, interview quotes, funnel numbers, CSAT, churn notes — into sense. Normally days of stitching; with AI in the loop I reconciled the qual and the quant in a fraction of the time, and drilled past the noise to one cheap, specific win rather than a vague "the product feels bloated".

Key takeaway
The big one: I wasn't up against a rival product, I was up against the WhatsApp group they'd set up on the side. Sort that out and the rest falls into place — and I didn't need anything clever, just the smallest fix I could stand behind. The guardrails kept me honest, and the prototype showed me "amber" is useless without a sense of how long you've been waiting.
What I'd do next
Run the 90-day pilot and sit tight until the read date. First job after: the gap the prototype found — a "time since sent" on every alert. If it clears the bar, bring back the demoted bits (live ack status, one-tap "Got it"), then work down the roadmap — compliance alerts, then two-way updates.
